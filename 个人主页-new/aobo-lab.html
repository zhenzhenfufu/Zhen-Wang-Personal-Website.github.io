AI for Neutrino & Dark Matter

Rare AI Lab · Prof. Aobo Li · Halıcıoğlu Data Science Institute & Department of Physics, UC San Diego

I joined the Rare AI Lab this October. The reason I wanted in is narrow and specific: physics is where a model that games its own metric gets caught, because the signal it is supposed to find is a real thing in the universe and not a number on a leaderboard.

What the lab works on

Machine learning for physics time series — the kind of data that carries evidence for dark matter, neutrinos and gravitational waves, buried in complex noise. Part of the group's current work is an evaluation protocol for long-horizon agentic ML development, motivated by a failure mode I had already run into from the industry side: when an agent optimises one or two numerical scores, it can take shortcuts, collapse to a degenerate solution, or game the evaluation procedure, and still produce a high-scoring model that is scientifically invalid.

Why it lines up with what I have done

At Pfizer I traced a review agent that was keying on how often a date appeared rather than on the rule it had been given — it would have scored well on anything but the right test. In my course planner, the model is not allowed to judge its own output; a deterministic checker does, and it names the rule that broke. Both are small versions of the same question this group asks at a scale that matters: how do you know the thing you built is actually right?

Where I am right now

Early. I am reading the group's current work and getting up to speed on the data; I have no results of my own here yet, and I would rather say so than dress up a month of reading. I am also taking DSC 291 with Prof. Li this quarter.
