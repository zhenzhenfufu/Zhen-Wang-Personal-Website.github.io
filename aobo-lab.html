AI for Neutrino & Dark Matter

Rare AI Lab · Prof. Aobo Li · Halıcıoğlu Data Science Institute & Department of Physics, UC San Diego

I joined the Rare AI Lab in September. The reason I wanted in is narrow and specific: physics is where a model that games its own metric gets caught, because the signal it is supposed to find is a real thing in the universe and not a number on a leaderboard.

What I am working on

The lab's open-source system is SIDERIUS, a long-horizon auto-ML agent built by Yue Ma with Prof. Li and the group: you give it a dataset and a research question, and it proposes models, implements them, trains and evaluates them, and uses what came back to choose the next attempt — declaring validity checks alongside the metric rather than optimising the metric alone. The write-up is here.

My work starts from that system and pushes on the training side of the loop. I am not rebuilding it; I am working inside it. There is nothing of my own to show yet — I joined in September.

Why the problem is shaped this way

Physics time series — the data that carries evidence for dark matter, neutrinos and gravitational waves — hide a weak signal inside complex noise. An agent that optimises one or two numerical scores on data like that can take a shortcut, collapse to a degenerate solution, or fit the evaluation procedure itself, and still report a number that looks like progress. The reason I wanted into this group is narrow: physics is where a model that games its own metric eventually gets caught, because the thing it is supposed to find is real.

What I bring to it

At Pfizer I traced a review agent that was keying on how often a date appeared rather than on the rule it had been given — it would have scored well on anything but the right test. In my course planner, the model is not allowed to judge its own output; a deterministic checker does, and it names the rule that broke. Both are small versions of the same question this group asks at a scale that matters: how do you know the thing you built is actually right?

Where I am right now

Early. I am working through SIDERIUS and the group's results, and running training on top of it; I have no findings of my own yet, and I would rather say so than dress up a couple of months of reading. I am also taking Prof. Li's DSC 291 this quarter.
