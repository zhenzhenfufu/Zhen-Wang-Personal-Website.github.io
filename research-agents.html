Evaluating Research Agents

With Dr. Zhen Wang (Assistant Professor of CSE, UT Arlington)

AI agents are starting to run experiments and draw conclusions. I work on two questions about whether we can trust what they report.

Rewarding evidence, not memory

FIRE-Bench (ICML 2026, by Zhen Wang et al.) asks agents to rediscover findings from published ML papers. A model may already know the paper's answer without looking at the data. I am turning such rediscovery tasks into a reinforcement-learning environment for small research agents, with a reward that credits only the conclusions that actually require the data.

My part: environment and reward design, training and evaluation runs on GPU clusters, and pre-registered comparisons.

Status: in progress, unpublished. The end-to-end training pipeline (GRPO with TRL and vLLM, answers isolated in a sandbox) is running, with multi-seed, pre-registered runs on open models from 3B to 27B parameters on H100 GPUs.

The observability gap

A person looks at a web page, a slide or a 3D model and sees what is wrong; a coding agent mostly sees the code, or a screenshot passed to a vision model. The idea came from Zhen; I am testing whether the gap is real and where it comes from, by giving model judges the same artifact through different channels (code only, image only, both, or a rendering tool) in pre-registered pilot studies, with a human baseline next.

Status: in progress, exploratory, not yet a paper. Two rounds of pilots are done; a pre-registered robustness study on SVG is running, and the human baseline comes next.

Results stay inside the group until they are written up.
